# Layer 1: Energy Companies Powering the AI Infrastructure Buildout

## Overview

The AI infrastructure buildout that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang describes as the largest industrial project in history is fundamentally constrained by electricity supply, particularly low-carbon, always-available power suitable for hyperscale data centers. Analysts expect global power demand to rise by more than one trillion kilowatt-hours per year through 2030, with AI-driven data centers accounting for a significant share of incremental load. This has triggered a multi-year capex cycle across utilities, independent power producers (IPPs), nuclear operators, natural gas suppliers, and clean energy developers in both the United States and China, including Hong Kong-listed names.[^1][^2][^3]

The following sections map investable, listed companies into four practical sub-buckets within Layer 1 (Energy): baseload clean power (nuclear and large diversified utilities), renewables and storage, natural gas and pipelines tied to AI power, and on-site / distributed generation (fuel cells and microgrids). For each bucket, key US and Hong Kong or China-related tickers are summarized in a table for easy extraction into investment workflows.

## Baseload Clean Power: Nuclear and Large Utilities

Nuclear power is emerging as a pivotal technology for AI-era baseload, as it combines high capacity factors with low emissions and firm output aligned with 24/7 data center demand. In the US, Constellation Energy has become a de facto AI–nuclear pure play, operating the largest fleet of nuclear reactors and signing long-term contracts with hyperscalers such as Microsoft and Meta. In China, CGN Power provides the main listed platform for nuclear generation via Hong Kong and Shenzhen listings.[^4][^2][^5][^6][^7]

US-listed leaders such as NextEra Energy, First Solar, and Fluence Energy are deploying gigawatts of renewables and storage, while Hong Kong-listed developers such as China Longyuan Power build large wind and solar portfolios in China.[^2][^5][^14][^15]

## Natural Gas, Pipelines, and Transitional Fuels

Given long permitting timelines and grid constraints, many AI data center projects are turning to natural gas as a faster-to-deploy, transitional fuel, sometimes combined with carbon capture. Large natural gas producers and pipeline operators stand to benefit as data centers locate near pipeline infrastructure to secure firm power supply, while US utilities plan new gas plants tied specifically to AI campuses.[^19][^2]

## On-Site and Distributed Generation (Fuel Cells, Microgrids)

As grid connection lead times stretch to years, data center developers are increasingly exploring on-site generation such as fuel cells and microgrids, allowing them to circumvent bottlenecks and gain more control over reliability and emissions. Solid oxide fuel cell providers such as Bloom Energy have seen sharp re-ratings as investors view their technology as a direct solution for powering AI clusters, while other hydrogen and fuel cell players remain more speculative or less directly tied to AI workloads.[^17][^22][^23][^24][^2]

### Key listed names – On-site power and fuel cells

| Region | Company | Ticker | Segment / Role | AI / Data Center Angle |
|--------|---------|--------|----------------|------------------------|
| US | Bloom Energy | BE (NYSE) | Solid oxide fuel cells for on-site power | Provides modular fuel cell systems that can supply reliable, always-on power to AI data centers; stock has surged on AI exposure.[^17][^22][^23][^24] |
| US | GE Vernova | GEV (NYSE) | Power equipment and grid technology | Supplies gas turbines, transformers, and smart grid software used in power plants serving AI data centers.[^25][^19] |

| US | Plug Power | PLUG (NASDAQ) | Hydrogen fuel cells | Fuel cell provider; as of 2026, has minimal direct AI data center revenue and faces skepticism over economics for AI loads.[^26][^24] |
| US | Microgrid and power services companies | Various | EPC, microgrid integrators, on-site generation developers | Benefit from trend toward behind-the-meter power projects serving data centers.[^2][^27] |
| Hong Kong / China | SinoSynergy Hydrogen Energy | 0963.HK (HKEX) | Hydrogen fuel cell systems | Hydrogen energy exposure in Hong Kong market; AI linkage remains early-stage and mainly thematic.[^15] |

## Hong Kong and China New Energy Issuer Ecosystem

Beyond specific tickers, HKEX is actively positioning itself as a listing hub for China’s new energy and decarbonization companies, with a growing pipeline of issuers across solar, batteries, hydrogen, and grid technology. By mid-2025, at least 11 new energy firms had submitted listing applications, adding to existing names such as China Longyuan Power, Xinyi Solar, Xinjiang Goldwind, GCL-Poly Energy, and various hydrogen or storage players. This provides investors with a broad opportunity set for constructing Layer 1 exposure on the Hong Kong market.[^14][^15][^28]

## How to Use This Layer-1 Company Map

The tables above can be extracted directly into spreadsheets or screening tools to build watchlists by sub-segment (nuclear, renewables, gas, on-site power) and region (US vs Hong Kong/China). From there, investors can apply their own filters for market cap, leverage, regulatory exposure, and sensitivity to power prices to tailor portfolios to specific strategies, such as:

- Long-duration core holdings in regulated utilities and nuclear operators leveraged to AI data center load.
- Higher-beta plays in renewables, storage, and fuel cells that may show greater sensitivity to AI-related news flow.
- Thematic baskets focused on Hong Kong–listed new energy names for China’s domestic AI and digital infrastructure buildout.

The intersection of AI and energy is still early, and company-level disclosures about AI-driven demand are evolving. A disciplined process combining top-down power market views with bottom-up company analysis will be necessary to separate durable beneficiaries from cyclical or hype-driven moves.[^23][^2]
