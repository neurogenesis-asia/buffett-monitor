# Layer 3: AI Infrastructure – Data Centers, Land, and Power-Adjacent Real Assets

## Overview

Layer 3 in Jensen Huang’s framework covers the physical and operational infrastructure that houses AI compute: powered land, data centers, shells, and operating capabilities required to turn Layer 1 energy and Layer 2 compute into usable AI services. Capacity for suitable land, power, and cooling is constrained globally, which is catalyzing large capex cycles among listed data center REITs, colocation providers, and regional data center operators, while private equity acquires a growing share of capacity.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]

This report focuses on listed US and Hong Kong/China names that own or operate data centers or related infrastructure benefiting from AI-driven demand. It organizes companies into three primary buckets:

- US-listed data center REITs and operators.
- Hong Kong/China–listed data center operators and hosting infrastructure.
- Adjacent listed players providing critical infrastructure (cooling, power management) heavily tied to the data center buildout.

## US-Listed Data Center REITs and Colocation Operators

US data center REITs Equinix and Digital Realty dominate the listed colocation market, operating hundreds of facilities worldwide and serving thousands of customers, including all major cloud and AI hyperscalers. Iron Mountain has successfully pivoted from document storage into data centers, while other tower and infrastructure REITs such as American Tower have meaningful data center portfolios.[^6][^7][^8][^9][^10][^1]

## Private Equity and the US Data Center Landscape

By 2025, private equity accounted for roughly 72% of total US data center investment, and about half of the top 25 US data center owners by active IT capacity were owned or controlled by private capital. Platforms such as QTS, CyrusOne, Aligned, Switch, DataBank, STACK, and CoreSite are no longer listed but remain important partners to hyperscalers and hardware vendors. Public investors can gain indirect exposure via listed infrastructure managers like DigitalBridge or through REITs and utilities that partner with these platforms.[^2][^14][^13]

## Hong Kong/China–Listed Data Center Operators

Hong Kong is a key regional data center hub, with 79 facilities, 8.2 million square feet of space, and 710 MW of capacity as of 2026, led by providers such as SUNeVision, Equinix, and Global Switch. On the mainland, GDS Holdings is a leading high-performance data center operator with dual listings in the US and Hong Kong, focusing on Tier 1 Chinese cities and expanding into Hong Kong and Southeast Asia.[^15][^16][^17]

## Hong Kong Data Center Market Structure

The Hong Kong data center market has 79 active facilities run by 39 providers, with 14 additional data centers under construction as of early 2026, reflecting robust growth driven by cloud and AI demand. SUNeVision (iAdvantage) is the largest provider by number of sites, followed by DayOne, while global players like Equinix, NTT, Global Switch, Vantage, and AirTrunk also operate significant campuses. This mix of local and global operators creates multiple listed and unlisted avenues for exposure to Layer 3 infrastructure in the region.[^19][^20][^17]

## Adjacent Infrastructure and Cooling Vendors

Beyond pure-play data center REITs and operators, several industrial and technology companies provide critical infrastructure for AI-ready data centers, particularly in power and cooling. Examples include Modine Manufacturing’s advanced thermal management systems, Eaton’s power management and UPS solutions, and other listed OEMs selling chillers, switchgear, and DCIM software into hyperscale and colocation sites. Hardware vendors discussed in the Layer 2 report (e.g., Lenovo’s Neptune liquid cooling, Nvidia’s networking gear) also straddle Layers 2 and 3 through integrated rack and pod-level solutions.[^10][^3][^25][^26][^27]

## How to Use This Layer‑3 Company Map

The tables above provide a structured starting universe for Layer 3 investments across US and Hong Kong/China markets. For portfolio construction, investors can:

- Use US data center REITs (EQIX, DLR, IRM, AMT) as core, income-generating exposure to long-term AI data center capacity demand.[^7][^8][^11][^6]
- Add select industrial names (MOD, ETN) that monetize the thermal and electrical upgrades required for high-density AI deployments.[^10]
- Combine Hong Kong/China data center operators (SUNeVision, GDS) and carrier-owned data centers (China Mobile, China Telecom, China Unicom, PCCW/HKT) for regional AI infrastructure exposure.[^18][^20][^17][^15]

Given the growing role of private equity, a full Layer 3 strategy may also consider listed infrastructure managers and REIT sponsors that own stakes in major private platforms, as well as utilities discussed in the Layer 1 report that control scarce power and land adjacent to AI-ready campuses.[^28][^3][^2]
